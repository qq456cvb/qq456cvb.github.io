Overview

9D pose of unseen objects, learned from synthetic models alone

CPPF estimates the 9D pose (3D position, orientation and size) of objects it has never seen, from a single RGB-D frame. Instead of regressing the pose, it lets thousands of point pairs vote for the object's center, orientation and size, using features that do not change when the object moves. Trained only on synthetic ShapeNet models, with no real images or pose labels, it transfers directly to real scenes.

How it works

Point pairs vote for the pose

For each pair of points, a network predicts a few numbers that pin down the object's pose up to one degree of freedom. One pair can only say "the center is somewhere on this circle", but thousands of pairs agree on one point. Try it: add pairs and watch the votes pile up.

Point pairs

1

A synthetic bowl with ground-truth voting targets, which the network learns to predict. Drag to rotate.

Invariant targets

Each pair predicts μ, ν (center), α, β (orientation) and a log-size offset. None of them change under rotation or translation, and symmetric objects need no special treatment.

Sign disambiguation

Symmetric shapes also vote for the flipped axis. An auxiliary classifier, which predicts whether each normal faces up or right, picks the right sign.

Coarse-to-fine voting

Vote once with all points, keep the points whose votes land near the winning center, and vote again. Inaccurate masks, or even plain bounding boxes, still work.

Sim-to-real by design

Only rendered depth of ShapeNet models with self-occlusion; points are voxelized and jittered on both sides. RGB is used only to tell a laptop's lid from its keyboard.

Abstract
Read the full abstract

In this paper, we tackle the problem of category-level 9D pose estimation in the wild, given a single RGB-D frame. Using supervised data of real-world 9D poses is tedious and erroneous, and also fails to generalize to unseen scenarios. Besides, category-level pose estimation requires a method to be able to generalize to unseen objects at test time, which is also challenging. Drawing inspirations from traditional point pair features (PPFs), in this paper, we design a novel Category-level PPF (CPPF) voting method to achieve accurate, robust and generalizable 9D pose estimation in the wild. To obtain robust pose estimation, we sample numerous point pairs on an object, and for each pair our model predicts necessary SE(3)-invariant voting statistics on object centers, orientations and scales. A novel coarse-to-fine voting algorithm is proposed to eliminate noisy point pair samples and generate final predictions from the population. To get rid of false positives in the orientation voting process, an auxiliary binary disambiguating classification task is introduced for each sampled point pair. In order to detect objects in the wild, we carefully design our sim-to-real pipeline by training on synthetic point clouds only, unless objects have ambiguous poses in geometry. Under this circumstance, color information is leveraged to disambiguate these poses. Results on standard benchmarks show that our method is on par with current state of the arts with real-world training data. Extensive experiments further show that our method is robust to noise and gives promising results under extremely challenging scenarios.
